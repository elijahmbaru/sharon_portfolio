The goal

Improve how facilitators deliver an existing menstrual hygiene curriculum.

WASH United works to end the sanitation crisis by teaching children and adolescents good water, sanitation and hygiene practices, including during menstruation. Nairobi Design Institute was asked to improve how the curriculum is facilitated by understanding the needs, challenges and pain points of running it.

My role

I consulted as a designer to redesign how the curriculum is implemented. I ran user interviews, prototyped and tested assets, and documented the process, findings and solutions for the client.

01 · Research

Sitting in on real sessions

What we did

We observed sessions run by different partners around Eastlands, Nairobi

In the first weeks we sat in on menstrual hygiene sessions and spoke with facilitators and participants about their challenges. This showed us how different facilitators plan and run sessions, and how they arrive at the key message.

Experience mapping

We mapped each session from both sides: facilitators and participants

From planning and getting to a session, through kick-off and content delivery, to tracking and follow-up sessions.

What we learned

The format of the sessions was getting in the way of the message

Class-style seating puts a “power figure” at the front of every session.

Boys are left out of the conversation because the topics are seen as sensitive.

Large groups mean a poor ratio of facilitators to participants.

Materials used during sessions are inconsistent.

Future sessions are poorly planned and tracked.

As a result, the curriculum is implemented inconsistently.

03 · Solutions

What we designed

MHM Roundabout Chart

A floor chart that works like a pizza, with everyone around it

Borrowing the concept of a pizza, the chart puts participants in a circle, each facing a slice of the topic.

  • More interaction during sessions, with no power figure at the front
  • Fewer participants per session, so everyone stayed engaged throughout
  • Room for boys and girls to take part in the conversation together

MHM Activities

Board-game activities for each topic, borrowing from Monopoly and Twister

We took game elements that resonate with the audience and designed an activity for each topic. They gave sessions structure, added some friendly competition, and were fun.

Impact 01

Participants stayed engaged throughout the session.

Impact 02

Teams collaborated and learned for themselves.

Impact 03

Facilitators felt more confident running sessions.
